How buying runs

Four steps from a request to a paid bill.

Asking, choosing a supplier, receiving and paying. Each one checks the budget and posts to the same ledger as finance.

01 · Requests

Request

Anyone can ask for what they need. The request reaches the budget owner with the remaining budget beside it, and an approved request becomes an order.

See it in a demo

  1. 01Raised by anyone

    From the office, the warehouse or the field.

  2. 02Budget shown beside it

    Spent, committed and left, before the decision.

  3. 03Approved by the owner

    Or sent back with a reason.

  4. 04Turned into an order

    Nothing retyped between request and order.

02 · Suppliers

Source

Quotes from approved suppliers are compared side by side on price, lead time and record, so the choice can be explained to anyone who asks.

See it in a demo

  1. 01Quotes requested

    From approved suppliers, on one request.

  2. 02Compared side by side

    Price, lead time and past performance.

  3. 03Chosen with a reason

    The reason is kept with the order.

  4. 04Order sent

    To the supplier, with your terms attached.

03 · Receipts

Receive

Goods are received against the order. Short, damaged or wrong items are noted at the dock, and stock updates in Inventory as they are put away.

See it in a demo

  1. 01Received against the order

    What arrived, line by line.

  2. 02Differences noted

    Short, damaged or wrong, with a photo.

  3. 03Receipt in one click

    The goods receipt note is created for you.

  4. 04Stock updated

    Inventory and the cost move together.

04 · Bills

Match and pay

Every supplier bill is matched to its order and its receipt before anyone can approve it. Only what was ordered and received gets paid.

See it in a demo

  1. 01Bill captured

    Read from the email or the scan.

  2. 02Three-way match

    Order, receipt and bill compared on price and quantity.

  3. 03Approved within limits

    Larger amounts go to a second approver.

  4. 04Scheduled for payment

    Handed to finance for the next payment run.
